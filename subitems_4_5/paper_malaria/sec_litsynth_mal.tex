
\section{Synthesis of the 197-Record Literature Audit}
\label{sec:litsynth}
We fetched and parsed 197 PubMed records on malaria diagnosis and imaging
(NCBI E-utilities; full list in the appendix). Classifying every record by
title and metadata:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{43/197} propose or evaluate a deep-learning model ---
architectures and scores are a large share of the field.
\item \textbf{5/197} address transfer or generalization across
microscopes, stains, or sites.
\item \textbf{0/197} state label auditing, label noise, or annotation
quality as a \emph{topic in their title or metadata}.
\end{itemize}
The third point requires an honest correction, discovered during external
review of this work: one record in our own screen --- Fuhad et al.\ 2020
(PMID 32443868), titled \emph{Deep Learning Based Automatic Malaria
Parasite Detection from Blood Smear and its Smartphone Based Application}
--- contains a manual expert label audit as a \emph{secondary
contribution}, invisible to a title-based screen. Its expert consultant
set aside 647 parasitized and 750 uninfected images as falsely labelled
or suspicious (27{,}558 $\to$ 26{,}161). A title/metadata screen
therefore cannot support the claim ``no prior audit exists''; the
defensible claim is narrower and, we argue, still novel: no prior
\emph{automated, estimator-based} census exists, no prior work publishes
machine-checkable per-image flags for this collection, and no prior work
analyses the \emph{directional structure} of the contamination. We state
the claim in exactly that form throughout, and we reconcile our estimate
against Fuhad's numbers in Sec.~\ref{sec:fuhad}.
